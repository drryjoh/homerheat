Does humid air carry the ball?

A little. Fly balls travel about 0.5–1 ft farther per +10 °C of dew point, the size physics predicts, because water vapor is lighter than the dry air it displaces. That is too small to show up in home run counts. Temperature, altitude and wind move the ball far more.

Home run hot spots are parks and climate, not humidity

One dot per ballpark, sized by games. The adjusted HR rate compares home runs with what each park's own air balls should have produced from their exit velocity, launch angle and spray. Question 2 · more home runs on humid days?

Not detectably, and the effect sizes explain why

Every factor below is on one scale: feet of extra carry from the same model, with the physics prediction beside it. Humidity is about a fifth of a 10 °C temperature swing and a twenty-fifth of Coors Field. Converting to home runs, +10 °C of dew point is worth about +1.9% (0.6 to 3.3%) among air balls. The game-level count, which does not hold exit velocity fixed, shows −1.1% (−3.3 to +1.3%), a null it was never powered to see.

Question 3 · rain

Rain adds nothing once you account for the cooler air

Home runs do get hit in the rain: 475 in the 264 games with rain or drizzle reported at first pitch. Rain games carry about 2.7 ft less in raw terms, all of it explained by temperature. With weather held fixed, every rain flag's interval spans zero.

Checks

The estimate holds across splits, and the placebo stays flat

Same primary model, re-run on subsets. Inside the sealed Tropicana dome, outdoor dew point should do nothing, and it does nothing. Closed retractable roofs leak outdoor air. Without a temperature control, the humidity estimate roughly triples.

Skepticism

What could still be wrong

  1. Quote the range, not the point estimate. The dew-point coefficient runs 0.69 to 1.36 ft per 10 °C depending on how temperature is measured. Humid evenings stay warmer during the game than first-pitch weather shows.
  2. ERA5 is grid weather. About 30 km cells at the scheduled first-pitch hour, with 10 m wind. Coastal parks can be biased, and wind effects are understated.
  3. The stored ball matters more than the air. A ball kept in humid storage is heavier and less lively. Going from 30% to 50% storage humidity costs about 14 ft and 25% of home runs, the opposite direction. MLB required humidors in all parks from 2022.
  4. Measurement breaks. TrackMan (2015–19) and Hawk-Eye (2020+) are fit separately. 2015 has exit-velocity gaps. Ball changes swing league home runs ±15–20%, about ten times any humidity effect.
  5. One pre-registered model. Only the primary distance model was fixed in advance. Terciles, splits, home run models, rain and maps are exploratory.
  6. The park map is not a humidity map. A park's extra carry includes altitude, fences, wind bias and climate.
Reproduce

Rebuild the data, the figures and this page

Each slide figure has its own script in figures/ that reads only analysis/results/ and runs in seconds. This page is built the same way by report/build_html.py.

python3 -m venv .venv && .venv/bin/pip install -r requirements.txt
.venv/bin/python src/fetch_statcast.py --years 2015-2025   # ~20 min first time, cached after
.venv/bin/python src/fetch_games.py --years 2015-2025
.venv/bin/python src/fetch_weather.py
.venv/bin/python src/build_dataset.py
.venv/bin/python -m analysis.run_all      # models + tables -> analysis/results/
.venv/bin/python figures/make_all.py      # slide PNGs -> figures/out/
.venv/bin/python report/build_html.py     # this page -> report/interactive.html
